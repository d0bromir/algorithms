# Fix CERTA's confidence where GRCh38 lacks copies

The literature offers no way to give a short read a biologically correct GRCh38 coordinate when its true copy is missing from GRCh38, and that is where nearly all of CERTA's remaining confident errors sit. For those mates the correct output is to abstain, so CERTA's 122 confident misplacements per million are won or lost in MAPQ ranking, not in alignment. Two bodies of evidence apply. On the model side, every widely used MAPQ (BWA-MEM, minimap2, strobealign, DRAGMAP/DRAGEN, Giraffe) is a function of the gap to the best competitor found in the reference and saturates near 60 when none is found. Only Bowtie2 penalises absolute divergence, and no aligner has a term for a copy the reference lacks. Our own stratification shows the competitor gap is still the strongest signal we have: 85 % of CERTA's confident errors sit in the 3 % of its confident mates whose BWA-MEM2 gap is below 50. That makes the top idea, which needs no download, a graded MAPQ fed by a competitor search that reaches past CERTA's 8-edit horizon and is calibrated on HG002. On the reference side, T2T-CHM13 carries the copies GRCh38 collapses (203 loci, 8.04 Mbp), and the published workflows that map to CHM13 and lift to GRCh38 (levioSAM2, FixItFelix) show gains in the same direction as our 42–59 % second-opinion result. The next line of work puts those copies into CERTA's index as sink contigs so the gain arrives in one pass, again with data we already hold. Population decoys, pangenome graphs and personal references report gains at the variant level and target other error classes, so they rank lower and need downloads. For `--bio`, the likelihood literature's gains come from noisy or divergent data, not from duplications. The fix there is a near-competitor floor plus a posterior that allows for an unseen copy. None of the sources found measures per-read placement of real reads against an assembly truth, and none puts a missing-copy prior into a short-read MAPQ, so both the measurement and the leading ideas are new.

## Every published MAPQ prices only the competitors the reference contains

CERTA's default MAPQ is BWA-MEM's single-end formula, and the source code explains why CERTA reaches 60 on mates GRCh38 cannot place. BWA-MEM sets the suboptimal score `sub` to the second-best hit it found. When it finds none, it uses min_seed_len × match score (19). MAPQ is then 6.02 × (score − sub), scaled by a length factor and by identity⁴, minus 4.343·ln(sub_n + 1), and multiplied by the fraction of the read not covered by repetitive seeds ([bwamem.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lh3/bwa/master/bwamem.c)). For a 150 bp read this comes to about **3.67 MAPQ per score unit, or roughly 18 per extra mismatch at the competitor**. Four mismatches of separation reach the cap, and a read with no competitor in the search space gets 60 even with eight mismatches against its best hit. BWA's pairing step can raise a mate by up to 40 more, which matters if CERTA ever adopts it ([bwamem_pair.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lh3/bwa/master/bwamem_pair.c)).

The other production MAPQs are built the same way:

- **minimap2 (short reads):** 40·(1 − x²)·ln(score ratio), times an identity factor. The identity factor multiplies a value far above the cap, and by our arithmetic a competitor at 90 % of the best score still leaves about 38 ([minimap2 hit.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lh3/minimap2/master/hit.c)).
- **strobealign v0.13:** min(60, ΔSW) for pairs, and 60 whenever only one candidate pair exists ([strobealign aln.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ksahlin/strobealign/v0.13.0/src/aln.cpp)).
- **DRAGEN:** Illumina documents the best-versus-second-best score difference as the main driver, with `aln-min-score` used as the suboptimal score when nothing competes ([DRAGEN MAPQ scoring](https://support-docs.illumina.com/SW/dragen_v42/Content/SW/DRAGEN/TPipelineMAPQ_fDG.htm)).
- **Giraffe:** a vg developer explains that mismatches count only relative to rival alignments, so a read with several mismatches and no rival can still score 60 ([Biostars](https://biostars.org/p/9581513)).

The one exception is Bowtie2's end-to-end table. It lowers a unique hit's MAPQ from 42 to 3 as the hit's score approaches the minimum valid score ([Bowtie2 unique.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BenLangmead/bowtie2/master/unique.h)). Li found Bowtie2 less prone to confident calls from paralogs missing from the reference, citing a two-mismatch pair to which BWA-MEM gave MAPQ 27 and Bowtie2 gave 6 ([Li 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.0929)). minibwa's preprint gives no MAPQ formula, although it attributes most false high-MAPQ alignments to large structural variants between individuals ([minibwa](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15357v1)). The mechanism behind minibwa's 48–56 grading of unplaceable mates is therefore undocumented and should be read from its source.

The probabilistic tradition states the limitation outright:

- **MAQ** normalises its posterior over positions in the reference, and its authors say the derivation assumes every read comes from that reference. On simulated human reads carrying mutations its MAPQs were too high. MAQ's own remedy was to fold the sample's sequence into the reference and remap ([MAQ](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/167/maq.pdf)).
- **LAST** computes a mismap probability as a softmax over the alignments it found, with an explicit horizon: alternatives below one-thousandth of the best alignment's probability are ignored ([last-map-probs](https://allstar.jhuapl.edu/repo/p1/amd64/last-align/doc/last-map-probs.html)).
- **Stampy** caps its posterior with a quality-derived P-value that the true candidate was never considered. This is the closest published analogue of a horizon term, but it covers candidates the search missed, not sequence the reference lacks ([Stampy](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/21/6/936)).
- **BWA-PSSM** is the only mapper found with an explicit term for reads that do not come from the genome, L·(1 − P(M))/P(M) in the denominator. Its alternative hypothesis is random sequence, which barely competes with a near-identical paralog ([BWA-PSSM](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4021105/fullTextXML)).

Learned recalibration has the same blind spot, through its training data. Qtip simulates reads from the reference, assumed that reads from missing sequence were rare, and found that 95.8–99.7 % of simulated errors were repeat ambiguity within the reference. On real NA12878 data it moved F-β by only about 0.001 ([Qtip](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5557537)). LoQuM and a 2020 logistic recalibration report similarly modest real-data gains ([LoQuM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3436835); [PeerJ 2020](https://peerj.com/articles/10501)). The literature therefore has no MAPQ term for the error class that dominates our measurements, and a recalibrator trained on reads drawn from GRCh38 cannot learn one.

Our own stratification supplies the missing number. Converting the error rates of CERTA's confident mates, binned by BWA-MEM2 score gap, into phred gives:

| BWA-MEM2 gap (AS − XS) | Error rate per million | Phred |
|---|---|---|
| < 5 | 117,000 | ≈ 9 |
| 15–20 | 6,100 | ≈ 22 |
| 40–50 | 564 | ≈ 32.5 |
| ≥ 100 | 2 | ≈ 57 |

Between gaps of 15 and 100, observed confidence rises by about **0.4 phred per score unit, or about 2 phred per extra competitor mismatch** (each mismatch costs five BWA score units). That is about nine times shallower than BWA-MEM's 18 per mismatch and twenty times shallower than a likelihood posterior with Q40 bases. This is our inference from a marginal stratification, not a per-read likelihood ratio. It pools reads from unique sequence with reads from missing copies, so what it measures is how often a read with a nearby paralog in GRCh38 actually came from a copy GRCh38 lacks. That is exactly what a MAPQ has to encode.

The gradient is steep enough that **85 % of CERTA's confident errors sit in the 3 % of confident mates with a gap below 50**, and CERTA cannot see it because its `sub` search stops at 8 edits and falls back to 19. Saturation also matters for the score itself. When much of an aligner's output is tied at 60, the pro-rata split at the matched-N boundary charges every tied mate the tie group's average error, the same convention as Qtip's within-group averaging ([Qtip](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5557537)). Any grading that breaks the tie therefore moves errors out of the top N. minibwa grades unplaceable mates at 48–56, and 88.4 of its 89 errors are on mates no GRCh38 aligner places. Since all five aligners fail on the same mates, that grading is the most likely explanation for the gap between CERTA's 122 and minibwa's 89.

Three experiments follow from this:

1. **Offline ceiling test (hours, no new CERTA code).** Re-rank CERTA's mates by the minimum of CERTA's MAPQ and an isotonic map from BWA-MEM2's AS − XS to the HG002 error rate, then re-score at matched N. This sets the ceiling for a gap-graded MAPQ and shows how much of it survives when lower-ranked mates refill the N slots.
2. **Move the signal into CERTA.** Replace the absolute 8-edit horizon with a relative one: the best hit plus 10–12 edits, which covers gaps up to 50–60. This follows LAST, whose horizon is defined relative to the best alignment. Alternatively, take an approximate `sub` from seed chains, as BWA-MEM does. Either way, count clipped local competitors, because BWA-MEM2's XS is a local score. A useful diagnostic is how many of the errors with gap < 5 have CERTA `sub` = 19. If many do, the competitor is a clipped partial paralog that CERTA's end-to-end edit search does not count. The certificate is unaffected: it concerns the best placement, and `sub` only grades confidence.
3. **Target the 15 % of errors (about 18 per million) with gap ≥ 50**, where no GRCh38 competitor is close. Apply a Bowtie2-style penalty on high-quality mismatches at the best hit, but only inside a locus prior, so that heterozygous reads in unique sequence are not penalised. The prior can come from the chain we already hold (if hs1ToHg38 maps two or more CHM13 segments onto one GRCh38 interval, that interval is a candidate collapse; this property needs checking in the file) or from GIAB stratifications. Aganezov's 203 missing-copy loci (8.04 Mbp) are its published counterpart ([Aganezov 2022](https://escholarship.org/content/qt18c391gh/qt18c391gh.pdf)).

The calibrate-on-HG002 rule means fitting this map on HG002 and freezing it before HG005 is scored. The leakage-free way to learn the joint effect of gap, divergence and locus prior is a Qtip-style model trained on reads simulated from CHM13 and mapped to GRCh38, with CHM13-only reads labelled as having no correct GRCh38 placement. Those training reads contain the missing-copy class that reads simulated from GRCh38 lack.

## T2T-CHM13 holds the missing copies, and sinks can bring them into one pass

The reference-side literature explains why a CHM13 second opinion removes 42–59 % of every aligner's confident errors while a second GRCh38 aligner removes 0–3 %: the information is in the sequence, not in the algorithm.

- **Aganezov et al.** used CHM13's haploidy as a truth. They found 908 problematic GRCh38 regions (20.8 Mbp, 73.6 % in segmental duplications), 203 loci (8.04 Mbp) with copies missing from GRCh38, and 12 falsely duplicated regions (1.2 Mbp). In the collapsed duplications, Illumina precision was 64.3–67.3 % on GRCh38 against 98.1–99.7 % on CHM13. Across 3,202 1KGP samples, CHM13 mapped 0.97 % more reads as properly paired than GRCh38, even when GRCh38 included ALTs and decoys ([Aganezov 2022](https://escholarship.org/content/qt18c391gh/qt18c391gh.pdf)).
- **Vollger et al.** found 16.5 Mbp of segmental duplication (SD) wholly absent from GRCh38 and a further 64.8 Mbp differing in copy number or structure. CHM13 matches the population median copy number for 94 % of SD bases, against 57 % for GRCh38. The same paper sets the main limit on lifting: only about 60 % of SD sequence has clear 1:1 orthology between the two assemblies ([Vollger 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/8979283/)).

### levioSAM2 is the published form of our second opinion

levioSAM2 maps reads to CHM13 and lifts them to GRCh38. It suppresses reads in CHM13-only sequence. It defers low-confidence lifts (BWA-MEM MAPQ < 30 and AS < 100, or targets in low-mappability regions) to a direct GRCh38 remap. It then reconciles the two by lower edit distance. On real 30x NovaSeq data it removed 23.9 % of GATK errors and 11.4 % of DeepVariant errors relative to direct GRCh38 mapping, and 35.2 % and 20.0 % in CMRG genes, using 55 % of the CPU time. On simulated chr20 reads the same pipeline gained only 0.08 % correct mappings ([levioSAM2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11610747/)).

Our own gap between real (42–59 %) and simulated (17–22 %) veto gains points the same way, and our truth design is a likely cause. Certifying real mates as unique on one HG002 haplotype drops repeats within a haplotype, which concentrates the real-read errors on missing copies. The simulated set keeps those repeats, and there CHM13 adds nothing. This is an inference; stratifying both sets with the GIAB v3.6 SD strata would test it.

Our veto only flags, and just 14–23 % of the mates it flags were misplaced. levioSAM2 suggests two cheap ablations:

- **Re-lift with the T2T consortium's nf-LO chain** (`chm13v2-grch38.chain`) instead of UCSC's hs1ToHg38. Vollger's 60 % orthology figure implies that some false flags are lift artefacts inside SDs.
- **Measure how often the lifted CHM13 placement of a flagged mate lands within 150 bp of the truth.** If it often does, adopt levioSAM2's commit rule. That turns the diverged-copy part of the error class into correct placements instead of abstentions.

### Sink contigs carry the same evidence in one pass

A sink index captures the same gain with one index and one pass. The precedent is old. hs37d5's 35.4 Mb of decoys halved CHM1's false heterozygous calls on GRCh37 by giving reads from missing sequence somewhere better to go ([Li 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.0929)). FixItFelix applied the idea to collapsed SDs using CHM13 sequence ([FixItFelix](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.07.18.500506v2.full)):

- Adding decoys for a handful of collapsed genes raised HG002's MAPQ-0 reads in those regions from 111,685 to 192,280. By construction, those extra MAPQ-0 reads come from the missing paralog, which is exactly what our veto removes.
- Masking false duplications cut MAPQ-0 reads in them from 358,644 to 103,392 and raised CMRG SNV F1 there from 0.012 to 0.980.

Three published design rules carry over, plus one warning:

- **Sinks must be ordinary contigs.** bwa computes a primary hit's MAPQ across non-ALT hits only, so a sink listed as ALT would not lower the competing GRCh38 hit ([bwa README-alt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lh3/bwa/master/README-alt.md)).
- **Mask what does not compete.** DRAGEN's alt-masking hard-masks ALT segments similar to the primary so they do not depress MAPQ, and keeps divergent segments, which act as decoys ([Illumina](https://www.illumina.com/science/genomics-research/articles/dragen-demystifying-reference-genomes.html)). Applied to CHM13, this means masking sink sequence that is near-identical to its GRCh38 ortholog.
- **Group haplotypes of one locus.** DRAGEN's multigenome mapper scores all haplotypes of a locus as a liftover group and takes MAPQ between groups, so alternative haplotypes do not lower MAPQ while real paralogs do ([DRAGEN](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10802302/)).
- **Validate every sink.** FixItFelix's FANCD2 pseudogene recall fell from 0.941 to 0.318 when a decoy stole real reads, so each sink needs per-locus validation against the HG002 truth before HG005 is used.

Our proposed construction, untested:

1. Take the CHM13 segments that hs1ToHg38 leaves unaligned, or that lift onto a GRCh38 interval already covered by another CHM13 segment. This mirrors levioSAM2's rule of aligning CHM13-only segments over 5 kbp back to GRCh38 to label them unliftable.
2. Exclude centromeric satellites, a large part of what CHM13 adds ([Nurk 2022](https://genomics.ucsc.edu/?p=222194)). They would absorb reads that no confident GRCh38 placement competes for.
3. Alt-mask the remainder and append it to the GRCh38 index as ordinary contigs.

A mate whose certified best hit lies in a sink is reported unplaced, or at MAPQ 0, in GRCh38, with its sink coordinate in a tag. The certificate then becomes a statement over GRCh38 ∪ sinks. The sink content is tens of megabases: at least the 8.04 Mbp of missing-copy loci, and at most the 81.3 Mbp of SD that Vollger lists as absent or structurally different. Against 3.1 Gbp, the index cost is marginal.

### Cheaper reference changes address other failures

Two cheaper reference changes rank lower because they fix different failures.

- **False duplications** in GRCh38 send reads to MAPQ 0, not to confident errors. Masking the five false copies on chr21 raised Illumina sensitivity in CBS, CRYAA and KCNE1 from 8 % to 100 % ([Wagner 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9117392/)). The NCBI analysis sets also hard-mask the chrY PARs and duplicated centromeric arrays ([NCBI README](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genomes/all/GCA/000/001/405/GCA_000001405.15_GRCh38/seqs_for_alignment_pipelines.ucsc_ids/README_analysis_sets.txt)). On our metric these changes mainly add confident correct mates and remove X/Y PAR scoring artefacts, so we expect a small gain at almost no cost.
- **Population decoys** add sequence GRCh38 lacks, but not the paralog copies it collapses. bwakit's hs38DH removed about 25,000 false positives while losing about 12,000 true positives ([bwa README-alt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lh3/bwa/master/README-alt.md)). Adding 46.6 Mb of common Chinese pan-genome sequence cut HG002 false positives by 3.3 % ([CPG](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8405635/)). In CMRG genes, hs37d5 removed 1,272 false positives but added 78 false negatives ([Wagner 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9117392/)).

## Graphs and personal references deliver variant gains on other error classes

Pangenome references report large gains at the variant level. None of that evidence is per-read placement, and the regions that matter most to us are the ones the evaluations set aside.

- The HPRC draft pangenome cut HG005 small-variant errors from 36,144 (BWA-MEM + DeepVariant) to 21,700 with Giraffe. That result used a DeepVariant model retrained on graph alignments, surjection to GRCh38, and ABRA realignment ([Liao 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10172123/)).
- Minigraph-Cactus found its CHM13-based graph beat its GRCh38-based graph even when calls were made on GRCh38 (CMRG F1 0.9830 against 0.9823; BWA-MEM + DeepVariant 0.9756). It did so after removing GRCh38's falsely duplicated and collapsed regions, because they "cannot be unambiguously projected" ([Hickey 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10638906/)).

That exclusion amounts to a published admission of our point. A graph can place a mate from a missing copy correctly in graph space, but in GRCh38 coordinates the only correct output is to abstain. A graph's value to us is therefore a better confidence signal, not a better coordinate.

Giraffe's 2025 release had the fewest MAPQ-60 errors in simulation and left more reads unmapped, every one of which BWA-MEM and minimap2 misplaced ([Giraffe 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12633584/)). Our run (94 against 89 per million, with 21,000 truth mates unmapped) fits that pattern, and suggests a test that costs nothing: count how many of those 21,000 mates minibwa places confidently and correctly. If few, Giraffe's abstentions are a veto signal like CHM13's. If many, they are lost sensitivity.

Our Giraffe configuration also gave up accuracy the literature documents. We used the GRCh38-based d46 graph without haplotype sampling. The literature favours a CHM13-based graph with diploid haplotype sampling, which had 9.1 % fewer variant errors than the frequency-filtered graph and lost accuracy as more haplotypes were sampled ([Sirén 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10760139/)). FORGe made the same point for SNP graphs: only 6–30 % of 1000 Genomes variants should be added ([FORGe](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6296055)).

The other population-aware approaches either rest on vendor numbers or target a different bias:

- **DRAGEN multigenome:** reports 47 % fewer SNP errors and 24 % fewer indel errors than its own linear reference, and a further 40 % reduction in v4.3. These are vendor figures, and they conflict with HPRC's finding that Giraffe beat DRAGEN 3.7.5 ([Illumina](https://www.illumina.com/science/genomics-research/articles/second-gen-multigenome-mapping.html); [Liao 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10172123/)).
- **Personalized references:** move F1 by tenths of a percent (impute-first on HG002: 99.77 % against 99.62 % for a linear aligner) and target allele-level bias rather than missing copies ([impute-first](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/36/4/740)).
- **Haplotype databases:** degrade when the sample has no close relatives in the database. Locityper's share of haplotypes at QV ≥ 33 fell from 94.9 % to 59.9 % when the sample's own haplotypes were removed ([Locityper](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.05.03.592358.full)). That held-out setting is the honest one for HG002 with the HPRC eval graphs.

Graph and HPRC-assembly experiments therefore rank as high-cost second opinions with uncertain gain. They are worth running only after the CHM13 sinks show how much error remains.

## `--bio` needs a posterior that admits a copy GRCh38 lacks

The likelihood literature shows where a better per-read model wins, and duplications are not among those cases:

- Quality-aware scoring raised correct placement of *D. melanogaster* reads on the *D. simulans* genome from 49 % to 66 % ([Frith 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2853142)).
- BWA-PSSM's damage and composition models raised sensitivity at matched PPV from 0.325 to 0.886 on AT-rich *P. falciparum* ([BWA-PSSM](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4021105/fullTextXML)).
- HipSTR and DRAGstr win with STR-aware indel models inside haplotype realignment ([HipSTR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5482724); [DRAGstr](https://gatk.broadinstitute.org/hc/en-us/articles/9570531065115-CalibrateDragstrModel)).

None shows a better likelihood resolving which of several near-identical copies a read came from, and MAQ's own calibration broke when the sample differed from the reference ([MAQ](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/167/maq.pdf)). `--bio`'s 138 against minibwa's 46 per million fits this. Its certificate holds and its optimum is the true maximum over GRCh38. But a posterior with Q40 bases turns each mismatch of separation into about 40 phred, the steepest curve of all the MAPQs above, in a stratum where our data put each mismatch at about 2.

Three fixes follow, cheapest first:

1. **Near-competitor floor.** Set `--bio`'s MAPQ to the minimum of its posterior and the gap-graded MAPQ from the first section. This guarantees `--bio` ranks no worse than the default mode while keeping the certified maximum-likelihood placement.
2. **MAQ's recommendation** to set the minimum per-base error probability to the sample-to-reference divergence rate ([MAQ](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/167/maq.pdf)). In loci where copies are missing, cap the evidence per mismatch at −10·log10(δ), about Q20, instead of the base quality.
3. **Phantom-copy term (the principled version).** Add π_∅(locus)·ℓ_phantom(r) to the posterior's denominator: the likelihood that the read came from an unseen copy at divergence δ from the best GRCh38 hit. Under that hypothesis each high-quality mismatch costs about δ (of order 10⁻² for recent SDs) instead of θ + ε (of order 10⁻³). This keeps BWA-PSSM's structure but replaces its random-sequence alternative with a paralog-shaped one. Reads that match the best hit perfectly keep their MAPQ. Reads with high-quality mismatches in copy-deficit loci lose it. Locityper's penalised null window, which absorbs read pairs no candidate haplotype explains, is a published precedent for such an option inside a likelihood model ([Locityper](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.05.03.592358.full)).

The prior π_∅ can come from CHM13 copy excess and from the sample itself. Li's maximum-depth filter, at about d + 3√d, removed many false heterozygous calls caused by paralogs missing from the reference ([Li 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.0929)). 23.6 % of CHM13's false heterozygous calls on GRCh38 fell at gnomAD excess-heterozygosity sites ([Aganezov 2022](https://escholarship.org/content/qt18c391gh/qt18c391gh.pdf)). A prior from sample depth also catches copies HG002 carries that CHM13 lacks, and it needs no external resource, at the cost of a second pass. δ and π_∅ are two parameters to fit on HG002 so that the posterior reproduces the slope of about 2 phred per mismatch. HG005 then tests whether they transfer.

The end point for biological correctness is assignment at the level of copies, not per-read scoring. Paralog callers pool every read aligned to any copy of a duplication, MAPQ-0 reads included, and decide at paralog-specific sites:

- Cyrius reached 96.5 % CYP2D6 concordance, against 84–86.8 % for earlier methods ([Cyrius](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7997805)).
- The SMN1/SMN2 caller reached 99.8 % ([Illumina SMN](https://www.illumina.com/science/genomics-research/articles/sma-carrier-screening-wgs-data.html)).
- ctyper recovers 96.5 % of phased variants, with at least 99.1 % correct copy number, across 3,351 CNV genes, from k-mers alone ([ctyper](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11326217/)).
- Parascopy learns which paralog-specific sites are reliable across samples ([Parascopy](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-30930-3)).

Read-cloud aligners showed that a joint model over reads beats per-read placement in SDs; EMA cut false positives by more than 30 % ([EMA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5989713)). For `--bio`, a second-pass EM over reads × copies of an SD family, with CHM13 sinks and a phantom among the copies, would let mismatches that recur across reads at the same offsets vote for an unseen copy. No per-read posterior can do that. SDrecall's authors caution that short reads cannot fully resolve SD ambiguity ([SDrecall](https://hub.hku.hk/handle/10722/372497)), and none of these tools reports per-read placement accuracy. This is a research project, best prototyped on a few FixItFelix genes (KMT2C, KCNJ18, MAP2K3, DUSP22) with data in hand.

### Nine of the twelve need no downloads

Rows 1–5, 7–9 and 12 need nothing beyond what we hold: the Ensembl GRCh38 primary assembly, hs1 with hs1ToHg38, the HG002 Q100 v1.1 haplotypes, the HG005 HPRC release 2 haplotypes, and the CERTA, BWA-MEM2 (GRCh38 and CHM13), minibwa and Giraffe BAMs already produced. Rows 6, 10 and 11 need the downloads named in the table.

Leakage decides which genomes can donate sequence:

- **HG002 Q100 haplotypes** serve only as truth and as the calibration target (the gap map, δ, π_∅, and sink validation). They are never sink sequence.
- **CHM13** is the only donor that keeps both samples clean.
- **HPRC eval graphs** exclude HG002, HG005 and NA19240 ([HPRC resources](https://github.com/human-pangenomics/hpp_pangenome_resources)).
- **HG005 haplotypes** can donate sinks for HG002 only in the row-9 diagnostic, because any method that keeps them gives up HG005 as a hold-out.

The HG005 truth needs our own dipcall run, because GIAB has no assembly-based HG005 benchmark. Mirroring GIAB's HG002 run, that means dipcall v0.3 with `-z200000,10000` and `hs38.PAR.bed` for a male sample ([GIAB v5.0q README](https://ftp-trace.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/ReferenceSamples/giab/release/AshkenazimTrio/HG002_NA24385_son/v5.0q/NIST_HG002_v5.0q_variant-benchmarksets_README.md); [dipcall](https://github.com/lh3/dipcall)). HPRC lists HG005's paternal and maternal haplotypes as trio-phased hifiasm 0.19.9 assemblies ([HPRC index](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/human-pangenomics/hprc_intermediate_assembly/main/data_tables/assemblies_release2_v1.0.index.csv)). Their labels will be noisier than HG002's, so HG005 scoring should be restricted to the dipcall `dip.bed`.

### Four guardrails keep a measured gain from being a scoring artefact

1. **Report more than one correctness criterion.** Alongside the 150 bp locus criterion, report a strict 20 bp endpoint that accounts for strand and clipping, as wgsim_eval does ([wgsim_eval.pl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lh3/wgsim/master/wgsim_eval.pl)), and the lenient interval-overlap ratio above 0.1 used by minimap2, paftools and minibwa ([paftools README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lh3/minimap2/master/misc/README.md)). Count chrX and chrY PAR placements as equivalent unless the chrY PARs are masked ([lh3 blog](https://lh3.github.io/2017/11/13/which-human-reference-genome-to-use)).
2. **Report the size of the MAPQ tie group at the matched-N boundary.** Interpolation hides the fact that an aligner whose MAPQ saturates cannot actually be operated at that N.
3. **Rule out projection circularity.** The truth is projected through minimap2 contig alignments, and minibwa uses minimap2-style chaining. Check that minibwa's lead is not an artefact of that shared method in ambiguous regions: restrict to the v5.0q benchmark BED and stratify by the GIAB v3.6 SD and low-mappability strata ([Dwarshuis 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11489684)).
4. **Validate downstream.** Call with DeepVariant and evaluate with `hap.py --engine vcfeval --gender male` against the v5.0q small variants and CMRG. minibwa's authors call a 528-FN difference out of about 46,000 SNP FNs comparable ([minibwa](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15357v1)), so a claim of improvement needs a change of several percent overall, or one concentrated in the SD strata.

If they are not already on disk, the guardrails need `HG002_GRCh38_v5.0q_smvar.vcf.gz`, `HG002_GRCh38_v5.0q_smvar.benchmark.bed`, `GRCh38_HG2-T2TQ100-V1.1_dipcall-z2k.dip.bed`, CMRG v1.00, and the GIAB v3.6 GRCh38 stratifications ([GIAB v5.0q](https://ftp-trace.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/ReferenceSamples/giab/release/AshkenazimTrio/HG002_NA24385_son/v5.0q/)).

## Conclusion

CERTA's remaining error is a calibration problem with a measured slope. Every MAPQ in the literature, likelihood posteriors included, was designed or trained on the assumption that the reference holds every copy. Under that assumption an extra mismatch to the nearest competitor is worth 18–40 phred. On real HG002 mates from copies GRCh38 lacks, it is worth about 2. Fitting that slope inside CERTA is cheaper than any reference change and should come first. The CHM13 evidence then measures what a better set of competitors adds on top. The order matters, because the two gains overlap, and the second is only worth its weeks of work if the first leaves much behind.

For `--bio`, a biologically correct placement of these mates cannot be a GRCh38 coordinate. It has to name the copy, and that copy lives in CHM13 or the pangenome. A CERTA that certifies over GRCh38 plus CHM13 sinks, and reports the sink copy in a tag, would make two certified statements: the best GRCh38 locus, and the fact that a better copy exists outside GRCh38. Every published aligner instead makes one uncertified guess. Neither a per-read benchmark of real reads against an assembly truth nor a MAPQ term for missing copies appears in the sources found, so the measurement itself is publishable, not only the fix.
