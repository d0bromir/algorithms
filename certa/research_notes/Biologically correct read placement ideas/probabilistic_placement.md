# Probabilistic read placement and mapping quality as a posterior (short reads, human, 2008–2026)

Context: CERTA `--bio` certifies the maximum-likelihood (ML) placement under an explicit model. It reports a posterior MAPQ. On HG002 2x150 NovaSeq X, scored at the locus (within 150 bp) at matched sensitivity, it makes 138 misplacements per million confident mates; minibwa makes 46. The certificate holds, so the remaining errors are cases where the model's optimum is not the true origin. They come mostly from copies missing from GRCh38. These notes collect what the literature models, how posteriors handle loci absent from the reference, and the quantitative evidence. Sources are cited inline. Unverified items are listed under Gaps.

## 1. Probabilistic aligners, likelihood-based realigners, and how accurate they are

### Takeaway
MAQ, Stampy, LAST, BWA-PSSM and Novoalign all compute MAPQ as a Bayesian posterior over candidate loci. They use base qualities plus a substitution and indel model. Every one of these posteriors is conditional on the set of loci the aligner examined in the reference it was given. BWA-MEM, minimap2 and Bowtie2-style MAPQs are empirical functions of the best and second-best scores, not posteriors. The best-documented accuracy gains from likelihood models come from three sources: using base qualities, data-specific substitution models (bisulfite, damage, AT-rich genomes), and STR/homopolymer-aware indel models applied in haplotype realignment (Dindel, HipSTR, DRAGstr). The gains are not in placing reads within duplications.

### Cited Findings
**MAQ (the origin of the posterior MAPQ)**
- MAQ defines mapping quality as Qs = −10 log10 Pr{read wrongly mapped}. The posterior is p(u|x,z) = p(z|x,u) / Σ_v p(z|x,v) under a uniform prior over positions. p(z|x,u) is the product of error probabilities at the mismatched bases. Minimising the sum of mismatch qualities is therefore the same as maximising this posterior. — [Li, Ruan & Durbin 2008, Genome Res (PDF)](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/167/maq.pdf); [journal page](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/18/11/1851)
- The exact sum over the genome is impractical, so MAQ approximates Qs as a minimum of two terms. The first is q2 − q1 − 4.343·ln(n2), where q1 and q2 are the summed mismatch qualities of the best and second-best hits and n2 is the number of second-best hits. The second term depends on k (the minimum number of mismatches in the 28-bp seed), the average seed base quality, and p1(k,28): the probability that a perfect hit and a k-mismatch hit coexist in a 28-mer. MAQ caps Qs at 99. — [MAQ PDF](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/167/maq.pdf)
- MAQ states that its derivation covers only the simplistic case where all reads are known to come from the reference and the ungapped alignment is exhaustive. — [MAQ PDF](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/167/maq.pdf)
- MAQ notes that real sample-vs-reference differences behave like sequencing errors. It recommends setting the minimum base error probability to the divergence rate. It warns that when the sample differs from the reference, the best position may be consistently wrong even with no sequencing error, which can invalidate the mapping qualities. As a remedy it suggests iteratively updating the reference with the sample's sequence and remapping. — [MAQ PDF](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/167/maq.pdf)
- Measured calibration: on simulated human reads with mutations, MAQ's MAPQs were overestimated (the true error rate was higher than predicted, worst in the 70–80 bin). Without simulated mutations the estimates were accurate. Using MAPQ cut false SNPs from 217 of 127,910 calls to 186 of 126,228, a 14% reduction. — [MAQ PDF](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/167/maq.pdf)
- MAQ's paired-end MAPQ: for a unique consistent pair, Qp = Qs1 + Qs2, assuming independent errors. If several consistent pairs exist, each read keeps its single-end MAPQ. Single-end reads in MAQ cannot exceed about 90. — [MAQ PDF](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/167/maq.pdf)

**Stampy (Lunter & Goodson 2011)**
- Stampy is a hybrid mapper: BWA places the easy reads and a 15-mer hash plus a gapped aligner handles the rest. The aligner respects quality scores and allows indels up to 15 bp. Stampy uses an approximate Bayesian model for the probability that a read or pair is mismapped. Its motivation is that mappers lose accuracy in the presence of sequence variation, especially indels. — [Genome Res 21:936](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/21/6/936); [PMC3106326](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3106326)
- Calibration as the authors report it: ELAND was systematically over-confident and Novoalign under-confident. Stampy, BWA and MAQ were well calibrated on that dataset for short and long, single and paired reads. — [PMC3106326](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3106326)

**Novoalign**
- Per a SEQanswers forum post, Novoalign's quality is a Bayesian posterior probability that the alignment is wrong, similar in principle to MAQ and scaled to resemble MAQ's MAPQ. This is not an official specification. — [SEQanswers thread](https://www.seqanswers.com/forum/bioinformatics/bioinformatics-aa/245-new-short-read-aligner?p=90890)
- A MAPQ-feature/recalibration study found that the aligners' own raw MAPQs, plus the rate of base-quality decay along the read, were the most useful features for predicting mapping correctness. Novoalign was the exception: its raw MAPQ was less informative. — [PMC3436835](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3436835)

**LAST (Frith and colleagues)**
- LAST's mismap probability normalises exponentiated scores over the alignments it found for a query: P(alignment i is the origin) = exp(s_i/t) / Σ_j exp(s_j/t). The scale t is printed in the lastal header, and mismap = 1 − that probability. The script assumes a single origin per query and makes no allowance for two or more alignments jointly reflecting it, as in splicing. — [last-map-probs documentation](https://allstar.jhuapl.edu/repo/p1/amd64/last-align/doc/last-map-probs.html)
- The default score cutoff for alternatives is the lastal threshold plus t·ln(1000). Each reported alignment has therefore been weighed against alternatives with as little as one-thousandth of its probability. This is an explicit horizon, and loci below it are ignored. — [last-map-probs documentation](https://allstar.jhuapl.edu/repo/p1/amd64/last-align/doc/last-map-probs.html)
- Frith, Wan & Horton 2010 put per-base error probabilities into the alignment scores so that sequencing errors and true differences are modelled together. Accuracy improved on simulated reads even when true differences were rare (about 0.2%). For D. melanogaster reads mapped to the D. simulans genome, correctly mapped reads rose from 49% to 66%. — [NAR 38:e100, PMC2853142](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2853142)
- LAST-TRAIN (Hamada, Ono, Asai & Frith 2017) fits substitution and gap scores to a dataset's own substitution, insertion and deletion frequencies, giving probability-consistent scores. It was shown on IonTorrent and PacBio and reported to reduce reference bias for nanopore reads. — [Bioinformatics 33:926, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btw742](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btw742)

**BWA-PSSM (Kerpedjiev, Frellsen, Lindgreen & Krogh 2014): a probabilistic mapper with an explicit "not from this genome" term**
- Each read becomes a position-specific scoring matrix. Entries are s(g|x_i) = log2(P(g|x_i)/q(g)) with P(g|x) = Σ_a P(g|a)P(a|x). The model combines Phred error probabilities, a mutation probability, and data-specific base-change terms (aDNA deamination, PAR-CLIP T→C). — [BMC Bioinformatics 15:100 (Europe PMC full text)](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4021105/fullTextXML); [doi:10.1186/1471-2105-15-100](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-15-100)
- The posterior for a match at ℓ is 2^{S(ℓ)} / [Σ_ℓ' 2^{S(ℓ')} + L·(1 − P(M))/P(M)]. L is the genome length and P(M) is the prior that the read originates from the genome (default 0.8). The extra term comes from an i.i.d. background model N for reads that are not from the genome, such as contamination or adapters. The sum is approximated by high-scoring matches above a threshold. MapQ follows the MAQ convention. — [BWA-PSSM full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4021105/fullTextXML)
- Results after MAPQ filtering, sensitivity at PPV, BWA-PSSM vs BWA:
  - ART 36 bp: 0.776 vs 0.735, both at PPV 1.000.
  - PAR-CLIP with T→C model: 0.662 vs 0.582, both at PPV 0.996.
  - Ancient DNA with damage model: 0.774 vs 0.703, PPV 0.997 vs 0.998.
  - AT-rich P. falciparum: 0.886 vs 0.325, PPV 1.000 vs 0.999.

  — [BWA-PSSM full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4021105/fullTextXML)

**Score-heuristic MAPQ (BWA-MEM, minimap2) and learned MAPQ (Qtip)**
- Minimap2's MAPQ is explicitly empirical: 40·(1 − f2/f1)·min{1, m/10}·ln f1. Here f1 and f2 are the primary and best secondary chain scores and m is the number of anchors. The paper reports that minimap2 rarely gives repetitive hits a high MAPQ, while other aligners sometimes miss close suboptimal hits and become over-confident. — [Li 2018, arXiv:1708.01492](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.01492)
- I found no primary source stating the BWA-MEM MAPQ formula. Community threads say it descends from MAQ's best/second-best logic, with the count of second-best hits lowering MAPQ (in the bwa aln case). — [Biostars 294075](https://www.biostars.org/p/294075/)
- Qtip (Langmead 2017) predicts MAPQ without depending on the aligner. It trains an ensemble tree model on "tandem" reads simulated from the genome to match the input's length, quality, gap and edit distributions. Its predictions beat the aligners' own MAPQs (Bowtie 2, BWA-MEM, SNAP) on average and at most thresholds. Repeat-driven ambiguity ("category 3") accounted for 95.8–99.7% of alignment errors in its simulations. — [Genome Biology 2017, PMC5557537](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5557537); [bioRxiv 103952](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/103952v2.full-text)
- A later study reports that recalibrating Illumina MAPQs improves SNP detection at low coverage. — [PMC7727374](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7727374/)

**Pair-HMM and haplotype realignment with context-dependent indel models**
- Dindel (Albers et al. 2011) realigns reads to candidate haplotypes with a Bayesian/probabilistic model of base-calling errors, mapping errors, and indel sequencing errors that rise in long homopolymers. Its gap-open-like parameter depends on the homopolymer run length of the haplotype, taken from a table. It was used for 1000 Genomes indel calls. — [Genome Res 21:961](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/21/6/961); [Dindel supplement](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/suppl/2010/10/26/gr.112326.110.DC2/dindel_supplementary_info_annotated.pdf)
- HipSTR (Willems et al. 2017) learns a stutter model per STR locus by EM. It then realigns STR reads to candidate haplotypes with an HMM, reports full STR haplotypes rather than lengths only, and outperformed local-assembly tools in the authors' tests. — [Nat Methods 14:590, PMC5482724](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5482724)
- DRAGstr in GATK: CalibrateDragstrModel estimates three per-sample tables: GOP (gap open), GCP (gap continuation) and API (a-priori indel/variant probability), indexed by STR period and repeat length. Defaults are a maximum period of 8 and a maximum of 20 repeat units. HaplotypeCaller reads them through `--dragstr-params-path`. — [GATK CalibrateDragstrModel](https://gatk.broadinstitute.org/hc/en-us/articles/9570531065115-CalibrateDragstrModel)
- DRAGEN (Behera et al. 2024) on HG002 small variants: F1 99.86% with 11,163 errors. DeepVariant+BWA had 2.49× more errors and GATK+BWA 6.07× more. Average error reduction was 82.88% vs GATK+BWA, 60.07% vs DeepVariant+BWA, and 44.33% vs Giraffe+DeepVariant. CMRG F1 was 98.64%, against 95.84% (GATK), 97.32% (DV+BWA) and 98.10% (DV+Giraffe). The main text I read defers the mapper's MAPQ and pairing details to the supplement. — [Nat Biotechnol 2024, PMC12022141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12022141)

### Inferences
- In the literature, a likelihood posterior's advantage is consistently about how evidence is weighted: base qualities, platform- or damage-specific substitutions, and STR-dependent indel rates. None of these sources shows that a better per-read likelihood resolves which of several near-identical copies a read came from. That matches CERTA's finding: the certified ML optimum is still wrong when the true copy is absent.
- Minimap2's (1 − f2/f1) and MAQ/BWA's n2 terms lower MAPQ for reads with many close competitors whatever their base qualities. A pure likelihood posterior with high base qualities can be sharply confident when the best GRCh38 copy beats the second by one or two Q40 mismatches. That is exactly the situation when the read comes from a missing third copy that differs from both. This plausibly explains why minibwa ranks better at matched sensitivity (46 vs 138 per million), even though `--bio` has the better optimum under its own model. It is testable by stratifying the 138/M errors by the number of high-Q mismatches and by the second-best hit count.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Stampy full text (PMC and Genome Research were blocked). Its numeric sensitivity and error-rate comparisons vs BWA, Novoalign, SHRiMP and others are not captured here, and neither is whether its posterior includes any term for reads from unreferenced sequence.
- Not verified in this pass: SHRiMP/SHRiMP2 (pchance/pgenome), BFAST, mrsFAST (all-mapper), RazerS 3 (full-sensitivity, error-rate based), GSNAP's MAPQ formula, Bowtie2's MAPQ table, GotCloud, DeepVariant's realignment. Their specifics need primary sources.
- DRAGstr's measured effect on homopolymer/STR indel accuracy: no head-to-head number found. Behera et al.'s main text did not describe DRAGstr.

## 2. Posteriors that allow for loci outside the reference (horizon, unseen competitors, reference incompleteness)

### Takeaway
Standard posterior MAPQs (MAQ, LAST, Stampy-style) normalise only over loci found in the given reference. MAQ says so explicitly. LAST adds an explicit horizon (alternatives within 1/1000 of the best). MAQ's p1(k,28) term caps confidence by the chance that a competitor was missed by the seed. The one explicit "alien" term found, BWA-PSSM's background model, is an i.i.d. random-sequence hypothesis, so it barely affects reads from a missing near-identical paralog. Separately, the literature quantifies how incomplete GRCh38 is, which bears on where reads from such paralogs end up. Neither T2T-CHM13 nor the HPRC pangenome is used anywhere as a prior in a posterior MAPQ that I found. That combination appears open.

### Cited Findings
- MAQ's mapping-quality derivation assumes all reads come from the reference. Its practical approximation includes a term based on p1(k,28), the probability that a perfect and a k-mismatch 28-mer hit coexist. In effect this is a prior for competitors outside the searched space. — [MAQ PDF](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/167/maq.pdf)
- LAST's mismap denominator contains only the alignments it found, down to t·ln(1000) below the threshold. It has no term for an origin outside the reference. — [last-map-probs documentation](https://allstar.jhuapl.edu/repo/p1/amd64/last-align/doc/last-map-probs.html)
- BWA-PSSM adds L·(1 − P(M))/P(M) to the denominator. This background model represents a read that does not originate from the genome. P(M) is the same for all reads (default 0.8). — [BWA-PSSM full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4021105/fullTextXML)
- How much GRCh38 is missing or wrong:
  - T2T-CHM13 corrects more than 9 Mbp of inaccurate GRCh38 sequence, including falsely collapsed or duplicated regions affecting 70 protein-coding genes. Across 3,202 1KGP short-read samples it removes tens of thousands of spurious variants per sample. False positives fall in 269 medically relevant genes by up to 12-fold, and more than 1 million additional high-quality variants are found. — [Aganezov et al. 2022 Science, doi:10.1126/science.abl3533](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abl3533); [CSHL repository record](https://repository.cshl.edu/id/eprint/40563/)
  - The HPRC draft pangenome adds 119 Mb of euchromatic polymorphic sequence and 1,115 gene duplications relative to GRCh38. For short reads, it reduces small-variant discovery errors by 34% and increases SVs detected per haplotype by 104% compared with GRCh38-based workflows. — [Liao et al. 2023 Nature (Google Research record)](https://research.google/pubs/a-draft-human-pangenome-reference/); [Nat Rev Genet commentary](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41576-023-00634-5.pdf)
  - Sherman et al. assembled reads from 910 individuals of African descent that failed to align to GRCh38. They found 296,485,284 bp in 125,715 contigs absent from GRCh38, about 10% more DNA than the reference. 387 of these contigs fall in 315 protein-coding genes. — [Nat Genet 51:30 (Arizona record)](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/assembly-of-a-pan-genome-from-deep-sequencing-of-910-humans-of-af/)
- DRAGEN incorporates corrections for GRCh38 errors. In nine falsely duplicated genes it reported 30,306 variants against GATK's 24,249. In collapsed genes (MAP2K3, KCNJ18), GATK reported 487 more variants, which the authors consider likely false positives. — [PMC12022141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12022141)
- Ebbert et al. 2019 systematically catalogue "dark" gene regions (poorly covered by short reads) and "camouflaged" gene regions (ambiguous, low-MAPQ alignments) in the reference. They assess how long and linked reads resolve them and give an algorithm for camouflaged regions. — [Genome Biol 20:97, doi:10.1186/s13059-019-1707-2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-019-1707-2)

### Inferences
- **A paralog-shaped alien term for `--bio`.** BWA-PSSM's term is the right structure but the wrong likelihood. For missing copies the alternative hypothesis is "this read came from an unseen copy at divergence δ from the best GRCh38 copy u*", not "random sequence". The posterior would become P(u*|r) = π·ℓ(u*) / [Σ_v π·ℓ(v) + π_∅(locus)·ℓ_phantom(r)]. Under the phantom hypothesis, each high-quality mismatch against u* costs about δ (paralog divergence, roughly 10⁻² for recent SDs) instead of about θ + ε_q (SNV prior plus base-error rate, roughly 10⁻³). So ℓ_phantom/ℓ(u*) ≈ Π_mismatches δ/(θ+ε) × (1−δ)^(matches). Reads that match u* perfectly keep high MAPQ. Reads carrying one or more high-quality mismatches in copy-deficit regions lose MAPQ sharply. CERTA's errors should concentrate in that class, and the certificate stays intact because it is a statement about the ML locus within GRCh38.
- **A locus prior π_∅ from external completeness resources.** Candidate sources are the CHM13-vs-GRCh38 copy-number excess (collapsed regions), HPRC gene duplications absent from GRCh38, and the false-duplication lists used by DRAGEN and GIAB. CERTA's most recent commit ("a MAPQ informed by T2T-CHM13") is consistent with this approach. Per the project's own tuning rule (calibrate on HG002, hold out HG001/HG005), any pangenome-derived prior should be checked for HG002 haplotypes to avoid leakage. Whether HG002 is in a given HPRC release must be verified; it was not checked here.
- **A sample-internal π_∅ with no external leakage.** Read depth, or k-mer depth of the read's own k-mers, above the expected diploid level at u* signals extra copies in the sample. MAQ's suggestion to update the reference from the sample and remap is the same idea at the reference level.
- **Learned calibration that includes missing copies (Qtip-style).** Simulate reads from a more complete genome (T2T-CHM13, or HPRC assemblies other than HG002), map them to GRCh38, and fit MAPQ on features. Candidate features: the `--bio` posterior, the best/second likelihood gap, the number of near-hits, high-Q mismatch count, and local copy excess. Qtip's result that repeat errors dominate (95.8–99.7%) suggests this is where calibration gains lie.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that puts an explicit "true locus not in reference" prior into a short-read MAPQ using paralog-like (near-identical) alternatives, and no population-frequency-weighted paralog prior inside a mapper. The literature handles the problem by changing the reference (decoys, CHM13, pangenome, DRAGEN multigenome), not the posterior. This gap was found within the limited search budget and should be re-checked before being claimed as novel.
- Not retrieved: the hs37d5 decoy rationale (Heng Li), bwa alt-aware postprocessing (bwa-postalt), GIAB/FixItFelix false-duplication masking, and Parascopy-style paralog copy-number models.

## 3. Population-variation-aware alignment and its effect on placement

### Takeaway
Variant-aware references (SNP-tolerant GSNAP, HISAT2 graphs, vg/Giraffe, DRAGEN multigenome, personalized pangenomes) reduce reference bias and downstream error. Adding variants indiscriminately also raises ambiguity: FORGe found that only 6–30% of 1000 Genomes variants should be added. The largest measured gains come from adding whole haplotypes and contigs (DRAGEN's 64 haplotypes, HPRC), not mismatch discounting at sites. Haplotypes and contigs are exactly what supplies the copies missing from GRCh38.

### Cited Findings
- GSNAP's SNP-tolerant alignment aligns to a "reference space" of major/minor allele combinations from dbSNP, so known minor alleles are not penalised. Overall yield barely changed, but placement changed for 7–8% of transcriptional reads, usually by revealing alternative genomic mappings. — [Wu & Nacu 2010 Bioinformatics (PDF)](https://ftp.sanger.ac.uk/pub/users/zn1/paper/gsnap.pdf); [doi record](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/fast-and-snp-tolerant-detection-of-complex-variants-and-splicing-in-short-reads)
- FORGe (Pritt, Chen & Langmead 2018) scores candidate variants for inclusion in a graph. One model weights by population allele frequency (population coverage), one by the variant's effect on repetitiveness (uniqueness), and a hybrid combines them. Adding more variants eventually lowered alignment rate and correctness. The best set was 6–30% of 1KGP variants and approached an ideal personalized genome. Tested with HISAT2 and an enhanced-reference approach. — [Genome Biol 19:220, PMC6296055](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6296055)
- vg (Garrison et al. 2018) maps to variation graphs with a generalized compressed suffix array. This improved accuracy over a linear reference and largely removed reference bias. — [Nat Biotechnol 36:875](https://www.nature.com/articles/nbt.4227.pdf)
- Giraffe (Sirén et al. 2021) maps to haplotypes threaded through a graph. It matched the most accurate previous mapper in simulation, reduced mapping bias, and improved DeepVariant SNV and indel calls compared with a single linear reference. — [Science 2021 (Google Research record)](https://research.google/pubs/pangenomics-enables-genotyping-of-known-structural-variants-in-5202-diverse-genomes/)
- Personalized pangenomes (Sirén et al. 2024) sample local haplotypes per 10-kbp block according to read k-mer counts, building a sample-specific subgraph. The authors report 4× fewer small-variant genotyping errors than GATK and a small DeepVariant gain. — [Nat Methods 2024, PMC10760139](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10760139/)
- DRAGEN's multigenome reference is GRCh38 plus 64 phased haplotypes from 32 samples, with established primary–secondary contig relationships. Alignments to secondary contigs are adjusted for MAPQ and scoring. It reduced SNV and indel errors by 54.20% on average for HG001–HG007. — [PMC12022141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12022141)
- The HPRC pangenome reduced short-read small-variant discovery errors by 34%. — [Google Research record](https://research.google/pubs/a-draft-human-pangenome-reference/)

### Inferences
- For CERTA's error class, the relevant population information is extra copies (haplotype contigs), not SNV alleles. One option is to add CHM13/HPRC sequence that has no GRCh38 counterpart as sink contigs, following the decoy, alt and multigenome pattern. The ML certificate is then issued over GRCh38 ∪ sinks. Reads won by a sink are reported to GRCh38 coordinates as unplaced, or with MAPQ derived from the GRCh38-vs-sink likelihood ratio.
- Caution for likelihood discounting at known variant sites: in segmental duplications, many cataloged "SNPs" may be paralog-specific variants. Discounting them would erase the very evidence that separates copies. FORGe's uniqueness criterion addresses this, but I found no direct source quantifying dbSNP/PSV confusion, so this is an inference.
- Sirén 2024 haplotype sampling is the closest published analogue of a sample-specific prior: k-mer evidence decides which population haplotypes, and therefore which copies, are present.

### Gaps
- Numbers not retrieved for GenomeMapper (Schneeberger 2009), BWBBLE (Huang et al. 2013), HISAT2 genotyping graphs (Kim et al. 2019), Graphtyper (Eggertsson et al. 2017), and Giraffe's MAPQ calibration.
- No study found that measures locus-level placement in segmental duplications for graph vs linear references on real reads against an assembly truth, like CERTA's HG002 Q100 projection. The 35.4% (segmental duplications) and 22.3% (satellites) HPRC breakdowns appeared only in a secondary summary and are unverified.

## 4. Joint placement of pairs and read clouds, STR-aware models, and left-alignment

### Takeaway
Joint placement helps by sharing evidence between reads. MAQ's paired MAPQ adds the two ends' MAPQs. Read-cloud models (RFA's Markov random field, EMA's latent-variable EM) place barcoded reads jointly and recover most segmental-duplication sequence. STR-aware indel models (Dindel, HipSTR, DRAGstr) are usually applied in haplotype realignment after mapping, not during placement. Left-alignment is a representation convention and does not affect where a read is placed.

### Cited Findings
- MAQ uses mate information to correct wrong alignments, add confidence, and place a read in a repeat when its mate is confidently aligned. It sets Qp = Qs1 + Qs2 for a unique consistent pair. — [MAQ PDF](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/167/maq.pdf)
- Read clouds (Bishara et al. 2015): the Random Field Aligner models reads from the same long molecule with a Markov random field. It recovers variation in 155 Mb of GRCh37 that short reads could not resolve, including 94% of 67 Mb of segmental duplications. About 6% of GRCh37 is inaccessible to short reads per 1000 Genomes. — [Genome Res 25:1570](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/25/10/1570)
- EMA (Shajii, Numanagić & Berger 2018) groups barcoded reads into clouds and chooses alignments across clouds by latent-variable optimisation. It combines edit distance with read density over fragments and outputs interpretable alignment probabilities. Genotypes from EMA alignments had more than 30% fewer false positives than Lariat on NA12878 and NA24385. — [bioRxiv 220236](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/220236v1); [PMC5989713](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5989713)
- STR/homopolymer models: Dindel uses homopolymer-length-dependent indel error, HipSTR per-locus stutter models learned by EM, and DRAGstr per-sample GOP/GCP/API tables by period and repeat length. — [Dindel](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/21/6/961); [HipSTR PMC5482724](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5482724); [GATK DRAGstr](https://gatk.broadinstitute.org/hc/en-us/articles/9570531065115-CalibrateDragstrModel)
- Left-alignment (Tan, Abecasis & Kang 2015): a VCF entry is normalized if and only if it is left-aligned (smallest position among equivalent entries of the same allele length) and parsimonious. In 1000 Genomes Phase 3, 2.3% of indels, 16.6% of biallelic MNPs and 90.9% of "other" variants needed normalization. — [Bioinformatics 31:2202, PMC4481842](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4481842)

### Inferences
- With plain 2×150 reads there are no barcodes, but RFA and EMA point to a usable principle: the reads covering a duplicated region carry a shared PSV pattern. A second-pass EM over reads × copies (including a phantom copy) would use mismatches that recur across reads at the same offsets as evidence for an unseen copy. A per-read posterior cannot do this. It is analogous to haplotype realignment (Dindel, HaplotypeCaller) but at the copy level.
- Left-alignment does not affect CERTA's 150-bp locus scoring. It matters only for indel-position comparisons inside STRs, where the model's cheaper STR/homopolymer indel opening ties placements along the repeat. Ties should be broken by a fixed convention, for example leftmost as in vt, before comparing to truth.

### Gaps
- MAPQ formulas for joint pairs in Stampy, BWA-MEM and Bowtie2 (insert-size likelihoods, mate-rescue posteriors) were not retrieved from primary sources.
- Long Ranger/Lariat details, and any read-cloud method evaluated against assembly truth in HG002, were not retrieved.

## 5. Evidence on whether likelihood-optimal placement is closer to the true origin than score-optimal placement

### Takeaway
Quantitative evidence that likelihood models place reads better exists for divergent, damaged or biased data: Frith 2010 (49%→66% correct for cross-species reads) and BWA-PSSM (matched-PPV sensitivity gains of 4–56 points). There is calibration evidence (MAQ, Stampy, Qtip), and variant-level gains from probabilistic realignment and graph references (DRAGEN, HPRC). I found no study that measures locus-level placement of real human reads against an assembly-derived truth comparing ML-optimal with score-optimal placement. CERTA's HG002 measurement (138 vs 46 per million at matched sensitivity, with the certificate holding) appears to fill a gap. It also shows that a better optimum does not give better ranking when the reference is incomplete.

### Cited Findings
- Using base qualities in alignment improved mapping accuracy on simulated reads with known origin. Correct placement rose from 49% to 66% for D. melanogaster reads mapped to D. simulans. — [Frith et al. 2010, PMC2853142](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2853142)
- BWA-PSSM vs BWA, after MAPQ filtering: ART 36 bp 0.776 vs 0.735 sensitivity at PPV 1.000; ancient DNA 0.774 vs 0.703; AT-rich P. falciparum 0.886 vs 0.325. — [BWA-PSSM full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4021105/fullTextXML)
- MAQ's MAPQ was over-confident on simulated human reads that contained mutations, because sample-vs-reference differences invalidate the posterior's assumptions. — [MAQ PDF](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/167/maq.pdf)
- The authors report Stampy's MAPQs as well calibrated, ELAND's as over-confident, and Novoalign's as under-confident. — [PMC3106326](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3106326)
- Learned MAPQ (Qtip) beats the aligners' own MAPQs, and repeat ambiguity accounts for most errors. — [PMC5557537](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5557537)
- Variant-level gains: DRAGEN's multigenome mapping plus ML calling gave 54.20% fewer SNV and indel errors from the pangenome reference, and 2.49–6.07× fewer HG002 errors than BWA-based pipelines. — [PMC12022141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12022141). The HPRC pangenome gave 34% fewer small-variant errors. — [Google Research record](https://research.google/pubs/a-draft-human-pangenome-reference/)

### Inferences
- The literature's gains from likelihood placement show up where the reference copy is present but the data are noisy or divergent. Where the true copy is absent, the gains come from changing the reference (CHM13, pangenome, multigenome) or from learned or heuristic MAPQ that penalises near-competitors. For `--bio` this points to keeping the certified ML placement and changing the posterior. Options include a phantom-copy term, π_∅ from copy-excess or CHM13/HPRC evidence, a minimap2/MAQ-style near-competitor penalty as a floor, and Qtip-style calibration trained on reads from missing copies. Together these would rank the 138/M errors below the confidence threshold without touching the certificate.
- Concrete test on existing HG002 data: for the misplaced confident mates, measure (a) high-Q mismatch count vs u*, (b) whether u* lies in a CHM13-collapsed or HPRC-duplicated interval, (c) depth excess at u*, and (d) minibwa's MAPQ for the same read. If (a)–(c) separate errors from correct placements, the phantom term should close most of the 138-vs-46 gap.

### Gaps
- I found no published head-to-head of ML-optimal vs score-optimal placement on real human reads with assembly truth at the locus. Stampy's numeric comparisons could not be retrieved and may contain partial evidence in simulation.
- I found no source quantifying how often the score-optimal (BWA) placement differs from the likelihood-optimal placement on Illumina human data, or which one is right when they differ.
- Within the call budget, I could not retrieve Frith & Kawaguchi 2015 (last-split, Genome Biol) or Frith's later work on scores as probability models.
