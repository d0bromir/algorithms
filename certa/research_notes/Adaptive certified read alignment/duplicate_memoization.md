# Memoization of duplicate reads and cross-read seed reuse in short-read aligners; duplicate rates in modern Illumina WGS

Context for the writer: CERTA's per-read result depends on (read sequence, hash of read name used only to tie-break among equally good loci). A cache keyed by sequence would have to store the *full set of tied optimal loci plus the certificate*, then redo only the name-hash tie-break, to stay byte-identical. Research date: 2026-10-23.

## Q1. Which aligners/accelerators deduplicate or cache reads or seeds, and what speedups are reported?

### Takeaway
Mainstream production aligners (BWA-MEM, minimap2; and by extension their GPU ports) map every read independently and do not cache across reads. The exceptions are niche or academic: Bowtie 2 / HISAT(-3N) have a seed-result cache that only pays off when reads are sorted or grouped. CORA (Nat Biotech 2016) and Masai (NAR 2013) exploit cross-read redundancy explicitly, with reported large gains mainly for *all-mapping*. Small-RNA pipelines routinely collapse identical reads before mapping. No primary source reports a speedup from exact-duplicate memoization alone in a best-mapper on human WGS.

### Cited Findings
**Bowtie 2 / HISAT family (seed-level cache)**
- Bowtie 2's `aligner_cache.h` says that "by caching the results of some alignment sub-problems, we hope to enable a 'fast path' for read alignment." It holds two linked multimaps: (1) "seed strings (i.e. read substrings) to reference strings", keyed by a 2-bit-per-base read substring plus its length; (2) reference substrings to the corresponding suffix-array elements. — [bowtie2 aligner_cache.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BenLangmead/bowtie2/master/aligner_cache.h)
- Each thread has a "current-read" AlignmentCache, described as a "local, unsynchronized cache". Higher-level caches "may or may not be shared among threads" (a "shared, synchronized cache"). The page size is 16 KB. — [bowtie2 aligner_cache.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BenLangmead/bowtie2/master/aligner_cache.h)
- Effectiveness is conditional: the cache "is particularly effective when the input is sorted or otherwise grouped in a way that brings together reads with (at least some) seed sequences in common." — [bowtie2 aligner_cache.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BenLangmead/bowtie2/master/aligner_cache.h). HISAT-3N carries the same header. — [HISAT-3N aligner_cache.h](https://mitra.stanford.edu/kundaje/marinovg/oak/programs/hisat-3n/aligner_cache.h)
- The Bowtie 2 paper describes only a per-read pipeline (seed extraction from the read and its reverse complement, ungapped FM-index seed alignment, SIMD dynamic-programming extension). It mentions no cross-read cache and gives no speedup figure for one. — [Langmead & Salzberg 2012, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/3322381)

**CORA: compressive read-mapping accelerator (Yorukoglu, Yu, Peng, Berger; Nat Biotech 34:374–376, 2016)**
- Citation and code: [doi:10.1038/nbt.3511](https://dx.doi.org/10.1038/nbt.3511); [CORA site](https://cb.csail.mit.edu/cora/)
- Mechanism: it identifies redundant k-mers across the read set and represents identical k-mers jointly as a single unit. These "read-links" are joined to a self-mapped reference *homology table* that holds all similar regions within a Hamming distance. Coarse mapping runs once per compressed unit and is then expanded. — [US patent 11031950](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11031950); [patent text (Justia)](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20160191076)
- CORA boosts an existing coarse mapper (BWA, BWA-MEM, Bowtie, Bowtie2, mrsFAST, mrsFAST-Ultra) and writes SAM. It supports all-mapping, best-mapping, unique and stratum modes, exact and Hamming-1–3 homology tables, and a "threeway k-mer compression mode". — [CORA site](https://cb.csail.mit.edu/cora/)
- Claimed gains: "sublinear-time paired-end read mapping … an order of magnitude faster" ([patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11031950)); "accelerate all-mapping by several orders of magnitude" (thesis abstract, [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/112879?show=full)). The acceleration "improves as the total size of the … read dataset increases" ([MIT group page](https://cb.csail.mit.edu/gems/)). The commonly repeated "10 to 1000X speedup of BWA" is a forum headline, not a verified paper figure ([Biostars](https://www.biostars.org/p/188229)).
- Stated motivation: existing aligners "process each read individually, potentially duplicating previous computations performed for earlier reads." — [patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11031950)

**Masai: multiple backtracking (Siragusa, Weese, Reinert; NAR 41(7):e78, 2013)**
- Masai searches all seeds of a read batch simultaneously over the FM-index, using an additional index built on the seeds. This "amortizes the cost of searching a large set of seeds by taking advantage of the repetitiveness of next-generation sequencing data." Reported speed: 2–4× faster than Bowtie 2 and BWA, and an order of magnitude faster than all-mappers (RazerS 3, mrFAST) at comparable sensitivity. — [NAR](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/41/7/e78/1068067); [arXiv preprint (2–3× claim)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4238v1)

**mrsFAST (cache-oblivious all-mapper)**
- mrsFAST is described as a "cache-oblivious short read mapper that optimizes cache usage"; its 2009 cache-oblivious rewrite was "2x faster in comparison to its predecessor." — [mrsFAST site](https://sfu-compbio.github.io/mrsfast/). mrsFAST-Ultra is up to 4.5× faster than mrsFAST and has a 10× smaller index. — [bioinformaticshome](https://bioinformaticshome.com/db/tool/mrsfast-ultra)

**GenStore and GenCache (SAFARI and Utah accelerators)**
- GenStore-EM (ASPLOS 2022) is an in-SSD filter for reads with at least one exact match in the reference. It reports that "on average 80% of human short reads map exactly" to the reference. It compares reads against a k-mer index *and a sorted read structure*, both scanned sequentially. Reported gains: 2.07–6.05× over software baselines (1.52–3.32× over hardware) for read sets highly similar to the reference. The approach does not apply to long reads. — [GenStore, arXiv 2202.10400](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10400)
- GenCache (MICRO) uses in-cache operators, Bloom filters and "improved data reuse" on top of GenAx. It processes a read batch in 37 s versus GenAx's 194 s (5.2×) with 8.6× better energy efficiency. It is in-cache *computing*, not memoization of read results; the full text could not be retrieved to confirm what reuse it exploits. — [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10121197-gencache-leveraging-cache-operators-efficient-sequence-alignment)

**Mainstream aligners: no dedup, but name- or index-hash tie-breaking like CERTA's**
- minimap2 maps each read independently in `worker_for`, with no result cache and no detection of identical sequences. Its tie-break hash is `hash = __ac_X31_hash_string(qname)` (unless `MM_F_NO_HASH_NAME`), then `hash ^= __ac_Wang_hash(qlen_sum) + __ac_Wang_hash(opt->seed); hash = __ac_Wang_hash(hash);`, and it is passed into `mm_gen_regs` / `mm_seg_gen`. — [minimap2 map.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lh3/minimap2/master/map.c)
- BWA-MEM aligns each read independently in `worker1`, with no read cache. Its tie-break is by the read's *index in the input*, not its name: `a[i].hash = hash_64(id+i)` with `id = n_processed + i`, and the sort order is score, then is_alt, then hash. — [bwa bwamem.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lh3/bwa/master/bwamem.c)

**Small-RNA and RNA tools that collapse identical reads**
- fastx_collapser "collapses identical sequences … into a single sequence (while maintaining reads counts)" — [nf-core module](https://nf-co.re/modules/fastx_collapser). It keeps no quality scores — [Bioinformatics paper PDF](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/handle/11380/1240339/339031/btaa051.pdf). miRDeep2's `mapper.pl` collapses identical reads before mapping — [Sanger ToL module](https://pipelines.tol.sanger.ac.uk/modules/mirdeep2_mapper). One user's small-RNA dataset collapsed from 7 million reads to 1,200 unique sequences with counts, and UMI information was lost — [Biostars](https://www.biostars.org/p/357944)

### Inferences
- The closest precedents to CERTA's idea are the Bowtie 2 seed cache (a sub-problem cache, locality-dependent) and CORA (cross-read compression plus reference self-homology). Neither is a byte-identical whole-read memo in a best-mapper. A sequence-keyed memo with name-hash re-tie-breaking appears to be novel or at least unpublished as such. I found no paper claiming it.
- minimap2 hashes qname (plus length and seed), and BWA-MEM hashes the read ordinal. Both therefore make tie-breaking a function of something other than sequence, which is exactly CERTA's situation. A memo can only be output-identical if it stores the full tie set (all equally good loci) and replays the same tie-break function. Caching only the winner would break identity.
- CORA's and Masai's big wins come from all-mapping and repeat-heavy seed search, where per-read work is large and highly redundant. That is the regime most similar to a certified exact aligner, whose expensive cases are multi-locus reads.

### Gaps
- Arioc, NVIDIA Parabricks (fq2bam), nvBWA, BWA-MEM2 and DRAGEN: I found no documentation of read- or seed-level deduplication or caching across reads. Parabricks and BWA-MEM2 aim for BWA-MEM-equivalent output, and I found no evidence of read-level memoization in them. "CORAL" could not be identified as an aligner; the likely intended tool is CORA.
- Bowtie 2's user-facing cache-size options (possibly `--local-seed-cache-sz` / `--seed-cache-sz` in `bt2_search.cpp`) were not verified, and no measured speedup of the Bowtie 2 cache was found.
- The CORA paper's own speedup table (Nat Biotech, paywalled) was not read. The figures above come from the patent, the thesis and the project pages.
- No paper was found that reports the speedup from memoizing exact-duplicate reads alone in a human WGS best-mapper.

## Q2. Typical exact-duplicate rates in Illumina WGS, and how they are measured

### Takeaway
Position-based (Picard) duplicate rates in modern PCR-free human WGS are around 9–14% of mapped reads: 9% mean in the 1000 Genomes 30× NovaSeq 6000 PCR-free set, and 13.7% ± 3.7% across the GIAB samples in Baid et al. A minority of samples exceed 20%. Patterned flow cells (ExAmp) add pad-hopping/optical duplicates that can dominate in some libraries. Exact sequence identity is a strict subset of position-based duplication for true duplicate fragments, because sequencing errors break identity. However, it adds identical reads from repeats and, for single-end keys, chance start-position collisions. I found no published WGS figure for exact full-length sequence identity, either per mate or per pair.

### Cited Findings
**Measured WGS rates**
- 1000 Genomes high-coverage (NYGC): TruSeq DNA PCR-free (450 bp), NovaSeq 6000, 2×150, about 30×. "The mean duplicate rate across the samples was 9% but there were 5 samples that had a duplicate rate greater than 20" (measured with Picard MarkDuplicates); median insert 433 bp. — [Byrska-Bishop et al., bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.02.06.430068v1.full)
- GIAB samples (HG001–HG007, including HG002) on NovaSeq, HiSeq X and HiSeq 4000, PCR-free and PCR-positive, 2×151, about 50×: "The proportion of duplicate reads was 13.7% ± 3.7%, with one outlier sample at 26.3%" (MarkDuplicates, via GATK 4.1.2.0, plus samtools flagstat). No per-sample, per-instrument or per-prep breakdown is given. — [Baid et al., bioRxiv 2020](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.12.11.422022v1.full)
- Same Hi-C library on two instruments: HiSeq 4000 (patterned) had 33% duplicates removed versus 2% on HiSeq 2500. 92% of HiSeq 4000 duplicate pairs lay on a single tile, versus 1% for HiSeq 2500, which implicates nearby-well re-seeding. Here duplicates were exact di-tag copies (2017). — [QCFail, Wingett 2017](https://sequencing.qcfail.com/articles/illumina-patterned-flow-cells-generate-duplicated-sequences/)
- WGBS (not PCR-free WGS): NovaSeq 23.8% duplication versus HiSeq X 19.0%, attributed to NovaSeq's higher cluster density. — [Sci Rep 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-46875-5)
- RNA-seq on NovaSeq 6000, NovaSeq X, AVITI and G4 (UMI-tools dedup with UMI plus position). At >125 ng input, "less than 7% of the reads" were duplicates. Below 125 ng, 34–96% were discarded. AVITI and G4 showed more duplication than Illumina below 15 ng. The paper does not separate optical from PCR duplicates. — [Zajac et al., bioRxiv 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.12.12.571280v1.full) / [Genome Biology 2025](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13059-025-03613-7)
- The Illumina NovaSeq X application note (NA12878, TruSeq PCR-Free, 2×151, 30×, DRAGEN 4.1) reports Q30, error and mismatch metrics but *no duplicate rate*. Read 1/Read 2 error rates: 0.25%/0.24% on NovaSeq 6000 and 0.15%/0.23% on NovaSeq X Plus. Mismatched bases: 0.48%/0.61% versus 0.36%/0.43%. — [Illumina app note M-US-00201](https://www.illumina.com/content/dam/illumina/gcs/assembled-assets/marketing-literature/novaseq-x-data-concordance-app-note-m-us-00201/novaseq-x-data-concordance-app-note-m-us-00201.pdf)
- An Illumina NovaSeq X versus UG 100 comparison downsampled NovaSeq X to "35× coverage depth (including duplicates)" and gave no duplicate rate. — [Illumina article](https://www.illumina.com/content/illumina-marketing/spac/ko_KR/science/genomics-research/articles/ultima-ug-100-vs-illumina-novaseq-x-wgs-data.html). The Tohoku Medical Megabank WGS QC paper reports no duplicate figures either. — [PMC12598290](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12598290/)

**Patterned-flow-cell (ExAmp) duplicates**
- Pad hopping is defined as "several adjacent pads being amplified from the same template", visible as contiguous same-colour patches. — [Illumina KEA patent family](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10808277)
- Anecdotal user reports: lower ExAmp loading raises duplication, and 350–450 bp inserts are recommended on ordered flow cells. — [SEQanswers NovaSeq thread](https://seqanswers.com/forum/sequencing-technologies-companies/illumina-solexa/60394-novaseq-from-illumina?p=289374); [same thread](https://seqanswers.com/forum/sequencing-technologies-companies/illumina-solexa/60394-novaseq-from-illumina?p=289370). On NextSeq, tile-edge duplicates were claimed by one user to dominate. — [Biostars](https://www.biostars.org/p/229842/)
- Index hopping, a separate artifact (misassignment, not duplication), occurs at about 0.1–2% on ExAmp instruments per a vendor blog. — [Revvity](https://www.revvity.com/blog/index-hopping-illumina-ngs-what-it-why-it-matters-and-how-unique-dual-indexing-solves-problem)

**How duplicates are defined and measured**
- Picard metrics: UNPAIRED_READ_DUPLICATES is "the number of fragments that were marked as duplicates". READ_PAIR_DUPLICATES and READ_PAIR_OPTICAL_DUPLICATES count pairs (optical is always ≤ all). PERCENT_DUPLICATION is "the fraction of mapped sequence that is marked as duplicate". ESTIMATED_LIBRARY_SIZE is based on paired-end duplication. — [Picard metric definitions](https://broadinstitute.github.io/picard/picard-metric-definitions.html)
- The optical-duplicate pixel distance defaults to 100 (unpatterned); 2500 is "more appropriate for the patterned flowcell models". — [Picard/GATK doc mirror](https://mitra.stanford.edu/kundaje/marinovg/oak/various/programs/gatk-4.1.0.0/gatkdoc/picard_sam_markduplicates_EstimateLibraryComplexity.html). Practitioners disagree for NovaSeq: 2500 may be too low, and Brian Bushnell suggests 12000 ([Biostars](https://biostars.org/p/9470759)); one pipeline recommends ≥10000 ([HCI docs](https://github.com/HuntsmanCancerInstitute/MultiRepMacsChIPSeq/blob/master/applications.md)).
- samblaster: "A duplicate read pair is defined as a pair that has the same signature for each mapped read", where the signature is sequence name, strand and 5′ reference offset (from POS, strand and CIGAR). "For pairs in which both reads are mapped, both signatures must match". Orphans match previously seen orphans, and doubly unmapped pairs are never marked. It needs about 20 MB per 1M read pairs. — [samblaster README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GregoryFaust/samblaster/master/README.md)
- fastp, by contrast, calls a read a duplicate "only if its all base pairs are identical as another one", so "if there is a sequencing error or an N base, the read will not be treated as duplicated" (see Q3). — [fastp README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenGene/fastp/master/README.md). In one plate-based single-cell UMI dataset, fastp reported 0.9% versus Picard's >32%. — [Biostars](https://biostars.org/p/9601763)

### Inferences
- **The upper bound on whole-pair memo hits for true duplicates is about 9–14% of reads** (Picard, PCR-free), and the real exact-identity rate is lower. Under a crude independent-error model with the Illumina PhiX error rates above, a 150 bp mate is error-free with p ≈ (1−0.0025)^150 ≈ 0.69 on NovaSeq 6000 and ≈ 0.80 on X (R1). A duplicate *pair* of two copies is byte-identical with probability ≈ (p_R1·p_R2)^2: about 0.23 on NovaSeq 6000 and about 0.32 on X. For a single mate it is about 0.47 / 0.64. Real errors cluster in a minority of reads, so these are pessimistic lower bounds, but they suggest exact-pair identity is well below the Picard rate.
- **Single-end keys hit more often than pair keys.** At 30× with 2×150 there are about 310 M pairs over about 6.2 × 10⁹ strand-positions. That gives λ ≈ 0.05, so about 5% of R1 mates share a 5′ start and strand with another fragment by chance, beyond true duplicates. These R1 mates are sequence-identical when error-free even though Picard (pair signatures) does not mark them. A per-mate memo therefore sees a higher hit rate than a per-pair memo, which matters if CERTA's per-mate search and certificate can be reused before pairing.
- **Speed ceiling.** If a fraction h of reads are cache hits and a hit costs almost nothing, the speedup is ≤ 1/(1−h). With h ≈ 0.05–0.14 that is about 1.05–1.16× count-weighted. The relevant quantity for CERTA is the *time-weighted* hit fraction. Duplicates of reads from high-copy repeats are rare per read but are exactly the reads with large tie sets and expensive certificates, so the time-weighted fraction could be far higher than the count-weighted one. It must be measured on CERTA's own profiles; I found no literature value.
- **Memory.** Exact-identity detection over a whole 30× run is expensive: fastp uses 1–24 GB hash buffers, and samblaster about 20 MB per 1M pairs, i.e. about 6 GB at 310 M pairs. For CERTA (around 21 GiB peak), an unbounded sequence-keyed memo is unattractive. A bounded cache keyed by a strong hash with full-sequence verification on hit, or a two-pass approach (count sequence hashes first, memoize only multiplicity ≥2), is more plausible. Duplicates are not adjacent in FASTQ order: patterned-flow-cell duplicates are mostly on the same tile but not consecutive.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives a per-sample duplicate rate for GIAB HG002 on NovaSeq 6000 or NovaSeq X. Baid et al. report only the pooled 13.7% ± 3.7%.
- No published, peer-reviewed NovaSeq X versus NovaSeq 6000 WGS duplicate-rate comparison was found. Illumina's app note omits duplicates.
- No official Illumina technical note quantifying pad-hopping duplicate rates was retrieved. Only forum anecdotes and patent definitions were found.
- No study measures *exact full-length sequence identity* (per mate or per pair) in human WGS, as opposed to position-based duplicates. This would need to be measured directly, for example by hashing R1, R2 and R1+R2 over a 30× HG002 run.

## Q3. FastQC "Sequence Duplication Levels" and fastp's duplication estimate

### Takeaway
FastQC estimates duplication per file (that is, per mate, single-end) from sequences first seen in the first 100,000 reads, truncating reads >75 bp to 50 bp. This inflates apparent duplication in deep WGS through prefix collisions and ignores pairing. fastp hashes whole reads or pairs for exact identity, which is closer to what a sequence-keyed memo would see, with about 0.01% hash-collision false positives.

### Cited Findings
- FastQC: "only sequences which first appear in the first 100,000 sequences in each file are analysed", and each is tracked to the end of the file for its count. Reads >75 bp are truncated to 50 bp because errors in long reads would otherwise inflate diversity. Sequences with >10 duplicates are binned. A headline figure estimates the loss from deduplication. It warns when non-unique sequences exceed 20% and errors above 50%. It assumes a diverse unenriched library and cannot separate technical from biological duplicates. Highly expressed RNA-seq, enriched ChIP and constrained-start libraries legitimately show high duplication. — [FastQC Duplicate Sequences help](https://www.bioinformatics.babraham.ac.uk/projects/fastqc/Help/3%20Analysis%20Modules/8%20Duplicate%20Sequences.html)
- fastp supports duplication evaluation and dedup for single-end and paired-end data ("reads/pairs"). It uses a hash to find identical sequences, and "about 0.01% of the total reads may be wrongly recognized as deduplicated reads" because of collisions. Evaluation is on by default and costs about 1 GB and 10–20% extra runtime. `--dup_calc_accuracy` levels 1–6 range from 1 GB to 24 GB (level 1 is the default without dedup; level 3, 4 GB, is the default with `--dedup`). Dedup has existed since v0.22.0. — [fastp README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenGene/fastp/master/README.md); paper: [Chen 2023, iMeta 2:e107](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10989850/)

### Inferences
- In 30× WGS, FastQC's 50 bp prefix identity on R1 files counts chance start collisions (about 5% at λ ≈ 0.05, see Q2) and repeat-derived prefixes as duplicates. It is not a good proxy for pair-level or full-length identity. fastp's whole-read or pair hash is the better quick estimate of memo hit rate. However, its 0.01% collision tolerance would be unacceptable inside CERTA, where a false hit is a correctness bug, so full-key verification is required.

### Gaps
- The fastp iMeta paper body (exact hash construction, pair handling, accuracy versus Picard) could not be retrieved (CAPTCHA). The details above come from the README.
- No published systematic comparison of FastQC, fastp and Picard duplicate estimates on human WGS was found.

## Q4. Beyond exact duplicates: shared long substrings and repeats, and cross-read reuse of seed lookups

### Takeaway
Cross-read sharing of seeds is universal at WGS depth: every genomic k-mer occurs in about 20+ reads. The obstacle to caching seed lookups is locality, because FASTQ order is random with respect to the genome. Published reuse schemes either require sorting or grouping (Bowtie 2 cache, GenStore's sorted read structure) or batch-and-join seeds (Masai, CORA, mrsFAST). The biggest redundancy sits in high-copy repeats: Alu about 11%, L1 about 17% of the genome; satellites 6.2% of T2T-CHM13.

### Cited Findings
- Repeat content: L1 occupies about 17% and Alu about 11% of the human genome ([bioRxiv 2024](https://biorxiv.org/cgi/content/short/2024.09.10.612283v1)), with about 1.1 million Alu copies ([Genome Research 2008](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/18/12/1875)). Satellite repeats are 6.2% of T2T-CHM13, with α-satellite the largest at 2.8%. This figure surfaced via an NSF PAR listing of T2T centromere work and was not verified against the paper. — [NSF PAR (Altemose et al. listing)](https://par.nsf.gov/search/author:%22Altemose,%20Nicolas%22)
- Seeding looks up every minimizer k-mer fetched from a read in the reference index, a memory-bound step. — [Mansouri Ghiasi thesis, arXiv 2608.31004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.31004)
- GenStore: about 80% of human short reads have an exact match to the reference, and its filter streams a k-mer index against a *sorted* read structure. Sorting the reads converts random index probes into a sequential merge. — [GenStore](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10400)
- Masai amortizes seed search over many reads "by taking advantage of the repetitiveness" of the data (multiple backtracking), and is 2–4× faster than Bowtie 2 and BWA. — [Masai, NAR 2013](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/41/7/e78/1068067)
- CORA jointly represents identical k-mers across reads and maps each unit once against a reference homology table. — [CORA patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11031950)
- The Bowtie 2 seed cache keys on read substrings (seeds) and needs grouped or sorted input to be effective. — [bowtie2 aligner_cache.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BenLangmead/bowtie2/master/aligner_cache.h)
- Related index-level work: a hash vector sorted by k-mer hash plus a lookup table gives cache-efficient lookups, while scattered seeds cause cache misses. — [Multi-context seeds, bioRxiv/Genome Biology](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.29.620855v3.full). FastHASH's adjacency filter (adjacent read k-mers must map adjacently) gives up to 19× speedup in an all-mapper. — [FastHASH](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257870544_Accelerating_read_mapping_with_FastHASH)

### Inferences
- k-mer coverage is C_k = C·(L−k+1)/L. For C = 30, L = 150 and k = 32, that is about 24 reads per genomic 32-mer. Almost every seed CERTA looks up will be looked up about 24 times in a run. But in FASTQ order the reuse distance is about the whole run, so an LRU seed cache would need to hold about the genome's worth of seeds; that is the index itself. Seed caching only pays off with reordering: minimizer or bucket sorting of reads (Clumpify-style), batch-and-join (Masai, mrsFAST, CORA), or a cache restricted to high-multiplicity seeds, which are exactly the repeat seeds whose hit lists are longest and costliest.
- The most promising CERTA variant is probably a *small cache keyed by high-frequency seeds or k-mers*, storing their hit lists or interval results, rather than a whole-read memo. Reads from Alu, L1 and satellites repeatedly query the same high-multiplicity seeds, which dominate per-read cost in an exact aligner. The memo hit rate would then track repeat coverage (tens of percent of reads touch some repeat seed) rather than the 5–14% duplicate rate. The size of this effect is not quantified in any source found.
- Whole-read identity from repeats (as opposed to duplicate fragments) occurs where repeat copies are near-identical over ≥150 bp, as in young Alu/L1 subfamilies and satellite HOR arrays. These reads have many tied loci, so a memo that stores the tie set saves the costliest certificates. Measuring the time-weighted hit fraction on HG002 is the decisive experiment.

### Gaps
- No published measurement was found of the fraction of WGS reads that share a ≥k-mer (or ≥50 bp exact substring) with an *earlier* read within a bounded window, i.e. the reuse-distance distribution under FASTQ order.
- No paper was found that evaluates read sorting or reordering specifically to improve seed-lookup cache hit rates in an FM-index or hash aligner. Bowtie 2's comment asserts the benefit but gives no numbers.
- mrsFAST's exact cache-oblivious scheme (whether it indexes reads and genome and joins buckets) could not be confirmed. The Nature Methods 2010 abstract was not retrievable.
- The 6.2% satellite figure could not be traced to a verified primary page. Alu and L1 percentages vary across secondary sources (about 10.6–11% and about 17%).
